# Street Repair Projects Evaluation - Analysis Summary

## Key Findings

### Overall Effectiveness
- **Total repairs analyzed**: 14,132 repairs (2016-2023)
- **Effective repairs** (PCI improvement ≥10 points): 24.8% (3,510 repairs)
- **Average PCI improvement**: 7.78 points
- **Median PCI improvement**: 0.00 points (indicating many repairs show minimal improvement)

### Effectiveness by Repair Type

| Repair Type | Count | Avg Improvement | Median Improvement | Effectiveness Rate | Avg PCI Before | Avg PCI After |
|------------|-------|----------------|-------------------|-------------------|---------------|---------------|
| **OVERLAY** | 2,900 | **24.85** | 23.04 | **73.0%** | 62.58 | 87.44 |
| **CONCRETE** | 104 | **18.21** | 13.97 | **62.0%** | 71.56 | 89.77 |
| **SLURRY** | 11,128 | **3.24** | 0.00 | **12.0%** | 84.26 | 87.49 |

**Key Insights:**
- **OVERLAY repairs are most effective**: 73% achieve significant improvement, with average 24.85 point gain
- **CONCRETE repairs are also effective**: 62% effective rate, but fewer projects
- **SLURRY repairs show minimal improvement**: Only 12% are effective, despite being the most common (79% of all repairs)
- SLURRY repairs typically start from higher PCI (84.26) than OVERLAY (62.58), suggesting they're preventive maintenance rather than major repairs

### Collision Reduction Analysis
- **Segments analyzed**: 13,447
- **Average crashes before repair**: 0.14 crashes/year
- **Average crashes after repair**: 0.23 crashes/year
- **Average change**: +0.09 crashes/year (increase)

**Note**: The increase in collisions after repairs is counterintuitive and may be due to:
- Confounding factors (traffic volume changes, weather patterns, reporting changes)
- Repairs often done on high-traffic/problem areas
- Temporary disruption during/after construction
- Need for longer-term follow-up analysis

### Repair Longevity
- **Average time to inspection**: 4.00 years
- **Average PCI degradation per year**: -0.84 points/year
- **Estimated years until 5-point drop**: 20.34 years (median)

**By Repair Type:**
- **SLURRY**: Degrades at 0.40 points/year (very slow, but starts from high PCI)
- **OVERLAY**: Degrades at 5.51 points/year (faster, but starts from much lower PCI)
- **CONCRETE**: Degrades at 3.45 points/year (moderate rate)

## Recommendations

### 1. Repair Type Selection
- **For severely degraded roads** (PCI < 65): Use OVERLAY repairs for maximum improvement
- **For preventive maintenance** (PCI > 80): SLURRY may be appropriate, but effectiveness is limited
- **For critical infrastructure**: Consider CONCRETE repairs despite higher cost

### 2. Effectiveness Monitoring
- Only 24.8% of repairs show significant improvement - this suggests:
  - Many repairs may be too minor to make a meaningful difference
  - Some repairs may be done too early (on roads that don't need them)
  - Quality control issues may exist
- Recommend stricter criteria for repair approval

### 3. Cost-Benefit Analysis Needed
- SLURRY repairs are most common but least effective
- OVERLAY repairs are most effective but less common
- Need cost data to determine optimal allocation

### 4. Long-term Monitoring
- Current analysis covers 4-year average window
- Recommend longer-term tracking (5-10 years) to assess true longevity
- Some repairs may show delayed benefits

## Limitations

1. **PCI Estimation**: Values between 2016-2023 are estimated, not measured
2. **Confounding Factors**: Collision changes may be due to many factors beyond repairs
3. **Data Quality**: Some repairs may have incomplete or inaccurate data
4. **Multiple Repairs**: Segments with multiple repairs complicate analysis
5. **Time Window**: 4-year average may not capture full repair lifecycle

## Future Analysis Opportunities

1. **Cost-Effectiveness Analysis**: If cost data available, calculate ROI by repair type
2. **Geographic Patterns**: Which neighborhoods/areas benefit most from repairs?
3. **Functional Class Analysis**: Do repairs work better on certain street types?
4. **Temporal Trends**: Has repair effectiveness changed over time?
5. **Predictive Modeling**: Can we predict which repairs will be most effective?
6. **Weather Impact**: How does climate affect repair longevity?
7. **Traffic Volume Correlation**: Do high-traffic areas need different repair strategies?
